## 1. Context and Why the Realm Mattered

Grün Berlin matters in the portfolio because it is not one website. It is a civic ecosystem around Berlin's public spaces and programs, where a public-facing promise has to hold up against a much messier operator and project-developer reality. The main platform communicates parks, projects, careers, press and news, annual reports, timelines, and a project map. Around it sit additional properties, campaigns, and channels with their own branding and technical needs.

That makes the case more important than a rebrand or a TYPO3 build. The digital layer had to stay understandable and trustworthy for the people of Berlin, while also staying maintainable across multiple civic spaces, marketing layers, and teams. The CV value is that this work sits exactly in the stretch between public-service communication and long-running ecosystem stewardship.

## 2. What the Real Complexity Was

Grün Berlin.de was the mother project and the real complexity center. It carried the heavy platform logic: career content, press and news, annual reports, timelines, project maps, berlin.de RSS feeds, TYPO3 templates, and an accessible frontend layer in Handlebars. On top of that, the wider ecosystem branched into additional surfaces like Infrasignal, Mauerpark, and Stadt Weide Land, plus newsletter and marketing work for other Grün Berlin properties.

The challenge was therefore not one isolated feature. It was keeping digital branding, UI, UX, frontend development, accessibility, TYPO3 publishing, and supporting channels coherent across many public-facing surfaces without pretending they were all one uniform brand or one single relaunch.

## 3. My Role and Ownership Boundary

My role was cross-functional delivery leadership across digital branding, UI/UX, and development. I was not the sole author of every brand or relaunch inside the ecosystem. The work was to translate multiple brand and content inputs into maintainable digital systems and to keep the ecosystem coherent as new needs, properties, and channels were added.

That included the main Grün Berlin platform, the accessible frontend layer, ecosystem extensions like Infrasignal, and supporting newsletter / marketing-design work for other properties. The hiring signal is not total authorship. It is the ability to hold together a civic ecosystem that would otherwise fragment across brands, teams, and tools.

## 4. Grün Berlin.de as the Mother Project

Grün Berlin.de is the strongest proof because it shows the full range of the work in one place. This is where content architecture, editorial publishing, public-service communication, accessibility, TYPO3 templates, and frontend implementation had to meet. The platform had to represent major Berlin spaces and projects while still remaining editable, maintainable, and visually coherent.

This is the case's main relevance for the CV: Martin can lead digital branding, UX/UI, development, and delivery on a civic platform where the visible interface is only the top layer of a much larger ecosystem.

## 5. Infrasignal as Operational Proof

Infrasignal belongs in the public case, but only for one reason: the traffic-light reporting system. It is the sharp operational proof inside the wider ecosystem. Citizens report a disturbance, the system identifies the right intersection, and service teams need links and data precise enough to act on.

That makes Infrasignal more than a spin-off website. It shows how the broader Grün Berlin work could turn public-facing reporting into an operationally useful civic flow.

## 6. Mauerpark and Stadt Weide Land as Ecosystem Extensions

Mauerpark and Stadt Weide Land should stay visible because they prove the ecosystem is broader than the mother project and sharper than a single design system story. Both required digital branding, UI/UX, and development, but within their own tone and public context.

They are useful proof because they show that the work was not only maintaining one central platform. It was extending an ecosystem of civic-facing brands and properties without losing coherence.

## 7. Supporting Channel Layer

The wider realm also included newsletter and marketing-design support for Campus Stadt Natur, Britzer Garten, Gärten der Welt, and Spreepark. These should not become equal pillars in the public case, but they matter because they show the real breadth of the ecosystem. The digital work extended into channels and campaign surfaces, not just websites.

That supporting layer helps explain why Grün Berlin belongs in the CV as ecosystem stewardship rather than as a single-site case.

## 8. Outcome and Hiring Signal

The hiring signal here is civic ecosystem stewardship. The work shows an ability to carry public-facing systems across multiple Berlin spaces and brands, while balancing branding, accessibility, editorial complexity, frontend implementation, and cross-functional delivery.

For the portfolio, the important read should be: Martin can take a public-facing ecosystem with many surfaces, stakeholders, and constraints, and make it more coherent, more usable, and more maintainable without overstating authorship or flattening the differences between the brands.
