# Tertianum / DPF — Narrative Draft

This draft is the working source for public portfolio copy. It merges three inputs:

- direct user clarification for the case emphasis and truth boundary
- `client-retrospectives/tertianum-dpf.md` for validated implementation detail and proof
- `data/resume.json` for factual consistency across the realm

It is intentionally longer than the site copy in `data/clients.ts`. The goal is to establish the correct narrative before compressing it.

## 1. Context and Why the Realm Mattered

DPF Group sits behind a client ecosystem where digital quality has to do more than look polished. Senior living, concierge services, hospitality, and investment all depend on trust, responsiveness, and a sense that the service on the screen will hold up in real life. In that kind of environment, a website is not only communication. It is also lead intake, reassurance, service design, customer management, and operational handoff.

That is what makes this realm more important than “keeping six sites running.” Tertianum and its premium residences needed contact flows that felt pleasant, personal, and age-accessible. RAS needed service communication and lead capture that matched a high-touch concierge promise. Brasserie Colette needed the same operational discipline in a hospitality context with a different tone and stack. DPF Investment had to hold the operator and investor layer together. The work mattered because these touchpoints shaped whether people trusted the service enough to take the next step.

## 2. Why the Work Was Harder Than It Looked

Most people underestimate this kind of account because they see separate websites and assume the work is mainly maintenance. The real difficulty was that marketing, SEO, CRM, customer management, tracking, security, and rollout were all equally relevant. A good decision for one of those functions could easily break another if the work was not connected.

That meant the job was not simply to design screens or ship tickets. It was to understand how Google Ads attribution, newsletter flows, contact forms, bot protection, CRM handoff, editorial publishing, and QA all affect the same service experience. Different brands had different identities and different technical setups, but the underlying expectation stayed the same: leads should be traceable, forms should feel trustworthy, spam should stay out, and teams should be able to change the system without creating regressions somewhere else.

## 3. My Role Across Brands, Teams, and Decisions

I was the accountable execution layer across that realm. Brand, marketing, SEO, HR, Google Ads, CRM, and customer-management needs all converged in the website layer, and I had to make those requirements compatible with each other. That included service design decisions, form behavior, tracking logic, rollout choices, security, documentation, and the practical question of how to keep the whole setup maintainable as it kept growing.

The work was therefore cross-functional in the literal sense. I had to speak several languages at once: the language of service quality, the language of lead generation, the language of SEO and attribution, and the language of delivery risk. The responsibility was not just to advise, but to stay responsive and accountable for what actually got implemented.

## 4. Tertianum and Premium Residences

Tertianum and Tertianum Premium Residences were the clearest proof that service quality and growth quality are the same problem. The forms were not just conversion elements. They were often the first serious contact point for people considering a residence or reaching out on behalf of someone else. That meant they had to feel calm, understandable, and personal rather than transactional.

Age-accessible contact flows were central here. The work touched lead handling, layout, validation, newsletter opt-ins, CRM handoff, and the broader infrastructure behind them. Microsoft Dynamics and Mailchimp were part of the operational picture, but the real goal was not tool integration for its own sake. It was to make sure contact, follow-up, and trust-building behaved like one system.

## 5. RAS Services

RAS made the service-design dimension even more explicit. Concierge services promise care, responsiveness, and reliability, so the website could not behave like a generic brochure. Service pages, contact flows, analytics, and CRM touchpoints had to support a nationwide roll-out without losing that personal-service character.

This is where the realm shows its range: not only premium residences, but also a service business where customer management and digital communication are tightly coupled. The public case should make that visible. RAS was not a side project. It is one of the strongest proofs that the work went beyond technical stewardship and into real service-system design.

## 6. Brasserie Colette and DPF Investment

Brasserie Colette should stay visible as an equal proof surface because it shows the same operational rigor under a very different tone. Hospitality asks for its own pace, brand expression, and conversion behavior, but the underlying requirement is similar: the digital layer has to feel trustworthy, maintainable, and commercially useful.

DPF Investment matters for a different reason. It connects the wider ecosystem at the operator and investor level. Keeping that surface coherent helps explain why this realm should not read as a random collection of brand sites. It is one ecosystem with multiple audiences and multiple business models, held together through long-term digital stewardship rather than one big relaunch.

## 7. Security, Monitoring, and Long-Term Maintainability

The long-running aspect of the work is part of the value. Since 2019, the ecosystem kept growing and changing: new campaigns, new concepts, HR needs, press work, CRM shifts, and different service requirements. The challenge was to adapt without turning each change into a new source of drift.

That is where security and operational resilience become part of the public story. Cloudflare Turnstile, bot detection, automated tests in GitLab pipelines, monitoring, Sentry-linked lead tracking, and cleaner alert separation all served the same purpose: keep the system trustworthy and observable while people keep using it. This was not infrastructure for its own sake. It was what made service quality and growth quality sustainable over time.

## 8. Outcome and Hiring Signal

The hiring signal is not just “WordPress for many brands.” It is continuity under complexity in a high-trust client ecosystem. It is platform thinking across several deliberately distinct brands. And it is the ability to hold service design, lead generation, CRM, SEO, security, and rollout together without flattening everything into one generic system.

For the portfolio, the important read should be: Martin can take ownership of a messy, growing, multi-brand digital ecosystem and make it more trustworthy, more maintainable, and more commercially useful over years, not just for one launch.
